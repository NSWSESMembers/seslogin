Perpetual Trophies

Eleven badges that belong to a unit, not to a member. Each one has exactly one holder at a time. Win it and you take it from whoever had it; the plate keeps every name that ever held it.

The cabinet

Each trophy, precisely

Category groups are the ones the personal badges already use in badges.rs, so "Storm", "Trainer", "Maintenance" and "Administration" mean the same thing everywhere. The Spanner and The Clipboard count hours rather than sign-outs, because one long afternoon in the shed is more work than three quick visits. The Owl uses no category at all, only the time a sign-in started, so it counts night call-outs however they were logged. Virtual categories (myAvailability) never count.

How a trophy changes hands

Standing record

The Boomerang · The Welcome Mat
  • The holder's claim is a number (days away, km travelled). Strictly beat it and the trophy moves on the spot.
  • Records sit in a 365-day window. When the holder's claim ages out, the best claim still inside the window takes over at the nightly run; if there is none, the holder keeps it.

Rolling lead

The Kettle · The Padlock · Iron Roster · The Owl · The Chalk · The Tarp · The Spanner · The Clipboard · Unbroken Chain
  • Each night, every eligible member gets a score over the trailing window.
  • A challenger takes the trophy only by strictly beating the holder's current score and meeting the minimum.
  • A holder who goes quiet keeps it until someone actually passes them, so the trophy is never empty.
  • The Kettle and The Padlock used to change hands every day, which made holding them mean nothing. Counting first-ins and last-outs over 30 days keeps them with the member who really opens and locks up the shed.

The plate remembers

Every change of hands writes a reign. The admin report shows it as an engraved plate, newest first: who held it, for how long, how many times they defended it, and who took it.

The same history is the fallback when a claim turns out to be wrong. If an admin deletes or edits the period a claim rests on, the nightly run hands the trophy back to the most recent earlier holder whose claim still stands, and records the reign as reverted rather than stolen.

House rules

One unit, one holder

A trophy is keyed by unit and trophy. There is no "joint holder" and no per-member copy.

Ties go to the holder

You have to beat the holder, not match them. Between challengers on the same score, whoever reached it first wins.

Never empty, except when the holder leaves

A trophy goes vacant only when its holder stops being a member of the unit (soft-deleted, or moved by member sync). The next winner takes an empty trophy with no minimum to beat beyond the trophy's own.

Same switches as personal badges

Only units with gamification on, and only scans on kiosks with gamification on, can move a trophy. Switching a unit off freezes its cabinet; switching back on resumes it as it was.

Home members only

Everything except The Welcome Mat is for members whose home unit is this one. The Welcome Mat is the reverse: visitors only. Guest sign-ins belong to nobody and never count.

Nothing rewards a long shift

No trophy is won by staying on site longer in one go. Iron Roster counts each sign-in at most 12 hours, the same cap the personal hour badges use, so a forgotten sign-out can't win it.

Under the hood

Storage

One new table, {prefix}_trophy, partitioned by unit so the whole cabinet is one Query. No GSI.

pk  location_id
sk  holder#<trophy_id>
      person_id, value, claimed_at,
      reign_started_at, defences,
      claim_period_id?   (omitted, never Null)
      version            (conditional writes)
sk  reign#<trophy_id>#<claimed_at, zero-padded>
      person_id, value, claimed_at,
      ended_at?, ended_by_person_id?,
      end_reason?  stolen | left-unit | reverted
      seen_at?     (set once the loser's kiosk scan told them)
  • Every holder write is conditional on version; a loser re-reads and re-applies, as badge_store::update already does for personal state.
  • Added to infra/dynamodb_test.tf, the prod tables and local-tables.rs together, plus SCHEMA.md.
  • Not audited, like person BadgeState: it is derived from periods. AuditingHandler passes the new methods through and says so.

When trophies move

  • On the scan, in the same place kiosk_badge_member already gates personal badges: The Boomerang (gap since the member's previous sign-in) and The Welcome Mat (reusing away_distance_km).
  • Nightly, inside badge_nightly::run, which already loads 53 weeks of each gamification unit's periods: all nine rolling leads, record age-outs, and a check that each on-scan claim's period still exists and still says what it did.
  • Nothing changes in Tally. Nightly trophies read every period whatever its source, so a sign-in made some other way still counts.

Delivery stack

One commit per PR, each safe to deploy alone. Members see nothing until PR 5; the first four can sit on prod quietly filling the cabinet.

Decisions to make

Should visitors be able to take home trophies?Proposed: no. Members on deployment would otherwise sweep The Kettle at whichever unit they're staying at. The Welcome Mat exists to give visitors their own.
Does holding a trophy leave anything permanent on the member?Option: the first time someone takes each trophy, they also earn a personal wildcard badge ("Once held The Kettle") in their own badge state. Proposed: yes, in PR 6, so losing a trophy still leaves a mark.
Show the scores, or only the holder?Proposed: the holder's own score is shown (on the plate and on the kiosk); a full leaderboard is not. It keeps the fun about the steal, not about grinding hours.
Which days count for The Kettle and The Padlock?Being first in on a day only you came in isn't much of a win. Proposed: only days with 4 or more members on site count, the same "busy day" threshold Lights On and Lights Off use. Check against prod how many busy days a typical unit has in 30 days before fixing the window.
Should The Owl count any night sign-in, or only call-outs?A member who drops in at 10:30 pm to finish paperwork would score the same as one paged to a storm job. Limiting it to operational categories would fix that, but brings back the problem of members not logging what they actually did. Proposed: any category, with the 30-minute minimum, and see whether it gets gamed.
